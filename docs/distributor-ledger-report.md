## 1. Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive review of the Distributor Ledger issued by Titan ERP for the account of Al-Madina Distributors covering the period 01 Jun 2026 to 30 Jun 2026. The audit was conducted against the screenshot of the generated ledger document and covers all visible data points including the KPI summary cards, aging analysis, and the transaction table.

**Six discrepancies were identified — three are critical, two are significant inconsistencies, and one requires clarification.** The most severe finding is a reversed balance formula: a recorded payment of PKR 200,000 causes the outstanding balance to *increase* rather than decrease. This is not a data-entry error; it is a systemic logic inversion in the balance calculation that renders the ledger's closing balance, aging schedule, and overdue amount unreliable.

Additionally, the two product line items visible in Invoice #SLIP-1005 account for only PKR 135,000, while the invoice states a total of PKR 500,000 — a gap of PKR 365,000 with no supporting detail rows.

**Until these issues are corrected, this ledger must not be used for credit decisions, debt recovery communications, or financial period closure.**

The balance column in the transaction table traces the net outstanding amount after each entry.

```

(PKR 750,000.00)          (PKR 450,000.00)        (PKR 650,000.00)
```

The arithmetic is internally self-consistent, but the *direction* of each entry is inverted relative to standard accounts receivable convention. A debit (credit sale) reduces the balance, and a credit (payment) increases it — the opposite of what is expected. This is the root cause identified in Finding 2.

## 3. Audit Findings

---

### Finding 1 — Product line items do not sum to the invoice total *(Critical)*

**Location:** Invoice #SLIP-1005, product sub-table and Total row

**Observation:**

The two product lines within Invoice #SLIP-1005 produce the following totals:

```
Dishwash Liquid 1L   50 cartons × PKR 1,800  =  PKR  90,000
Fabric Softener 1L   30 cartons × PKR 1,500  =  PKR  45,000
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Line item total                              =  PKR 135,000

Stated Total (Cash + Credit)                 =  PKR 500,000
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Unexplained gap                              =  PKR 365,000
```

**Impact:**

PKR 365,000 worth of goods (or charges) has no corresponding line item in the ledger. This means:

- The client cannot verify what they are being charged for.
- The invoice is incomplete for audit or dispute purposes.
- Any tax computation (GST/sales tax) based on the line items will be incorrect.

**Possible causes:**

- Additional product rows exist in the database but the rendering query is incomplete or truncated.
- A portion of the invoice value represents handling, delivery, or tax charges not broken out as line items.
- A data mapping error is causing only the first two rows to render while others are silently dropped.

**Required action:** Retrieve the full invoice record from the database and confirm all line items. The rendered ledger must display every component that contributes to the PKR 500,000 total.

---

### Finding 2 — Payment increases the outstanding balance instead of decreasing it *(Critical)*

**Location:** Payment row, Balance column

**Observation:**

In any standard accounts receivable (AR) ledger, a payment received from a customer reduces the amount they owe. The observed behavior is the exact opposite:

```
Balance before payment    =  (PKR 450,000)   [amount owed to Titan]
Payment received          =   PKR 200,000
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Expected balance after    =  (PKR 250,000)   [450,000 – 200,000]
Actual balance shown      =  (PKR 650,000)   [450,000 + 200,000]  ✗
```

The balance increased by PKR 200,000 when it should have decreased by PKR 200,000. The same inversion applies to the invoice debit entry: adding a credit sale of PKR 300,000 reduced the balance from (750,000) to (450,000) instead of increasing it.

**The effective formula being applied:**

```
Balance(t) = Balance(t–1) + Debit – Credit
```

Where Opening Balance = –750,000 (negative = Titan owes distributor):

```
After invoice:   –750,000 + 300,000           = –450,000  ✓ (math checks out)
After payment:   –450,000 – 200,000           = –650,000  ✓ (math checks out)
```

The arithmetic is self-consistent, but it implies the opening balance is a *credit balance in the distributor's favour* (i.e., Titan owes Al-Madina), and that the debit column represents *goods consumed against that advance*, while the credit column represents *new advances paid in by the distributor*. This is the accounting logic of a prepayment/advance ledger, not a trade receivable ledger — and it contradicts the column header labeling of "DEBIT (CREDIT SALE)" and "CREDIT (PAYMENT)".

**Impact:**

- The ledger cannot be used to communicate how much the distributor owes.
- Statements sent to Al-Madina based on this ledger will be factually incorrect.
- Any automated dunning or credit limit enforcement will act on wrong data.

**Required action:** Audit the balance computation logic in the backend. Determine whether this is an AR (receivable) ledger or an AP/advance (payable) ledger, and ensure the formula, column headers, and sign conventions are all consistent with that intent.

---

### Finding 3 — Closing balance is not derivable from the summary KPIs using any standard formula *(Critical)*

**Location:** Summary KPI cards — Opening Balance, Period Sales, Period Payments, Closing Balance

**Observation:**

The standard AR closing balance formula is:

```
Closing Balance = Opening Balance + Period Sales – Period Payments
               = 750,000 + 500,000 – 200,000
               = 1,050,000  ✗  (actual: 650,000)
```

Accounting for immediate cash collection (only credit sales create a receivable):

```
Closing Balance = Opening Balance + Period Credit – Period Payments
               = 750,000 + 300,000 – 200,000
               = 850,000  ✗  (actual: 650,000)
```

The only arithmetic path to the stated closing balance of PKR 650,000 is:

```
Closing Balance = Opening Balance – Period Credit + Period Payments
               = 750,000 – 300,000 + 200,000
               = 650,000  ✓  (but formula is inverted)
```

This inverted formula is consistent with Finding 2 — the balance formula subtracts sales and adds payments, which is the reverse of standard AR. No conventional accounting formula yields PKR 650,000 from these inputs.

**Impact:**

- Period-end reconciliation against the general ledger will fail.
- The closing balance cannot be independently verified by the client or an auditor.
- Trust in the ERP's financial reporting is undermined.

**Required action:** Correct the balance formula and recompute the closing balance. Cross-verify the corrected figure against all transaction entries and the general ledger.

---

### Finding 4 — Aging totals do not match the closing balance *(Critical)*

**Location:** Aging Analysis section vs. Closing Balance KPI card

**Observation:**

The aging schedule is meant to show how the full outstanding balance is distributed across overdue time buckets. The sum of all aging buckets must equal the total outstanding receivable (closing balance).

```
Current (not yet due)    =  PKR 100,000
1–30 Days                =  PKR       0
31–60 Days               =  PKR       0
61–90 Days               =  PKR       0
90+ Days                 =  PKR       0
─────────────────────────────────────────
Aging total              =  PKR 100,000

Closing balance          =  PKR 650,000
─────────────────────────────────────────
Amount missing from aging =  PKR 550,000  ✗
```

The PKR 550,000 gap corresponds approximately to the opening balance of PKR 750,000 minus the credit sale of PKR 300,000 that was partially applied against it. This suggests the aging algorithm is only evaluating transactions created within the current period and is not picking up the carried-forward balance from prior periods.

**Impact:**

- The aging schedule is misleading — it significantly understates the overdue exposure.
- Credit risk assessment based on this schedule will be dangerously inaccurate.
- Accounts appearing "current" or "low risk" may in fact have significant historical overdue amounts.

**Required action:** Modify the aging query to include the brought-forward opening balance and allocate it to the appropriate aging bucket (likely 90+ days, given it predates the current period).

---

### Finding 5 — Overdue Amount contradicts all aging buckets *(Inconsistency)*

**Location:** Overdue Amount KPI card vs. Aging Analysis section

**Observation:**

The Overdue Amount KPI card explicitly states **PKR 300,000**. However, every single overdue aging bucket (1–30, 31–60, 61–90, and 90+ days) shows PKR 0.00. These two data points are sourced from different parts of the system and are directly contradicting each other within the same report.

```
Overdue Amount (KPI card)        =  PKR 300,000
Sum of all overdue aging buckets =  PKR       0
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
Contradiction                    =  PKR 300,000  ✗
```

**Possible causes:**

- The Overdue Amount KPI card and the aging breakdown query use different date cutoffs or different base tables.
- The aging algorithm has a filtering bug that zeroes out all non-current buckets regardless of actual data.
- The Overdue Amount figure is hard-coded or derived from a separate (possibly correct) source, while the aging table is broken.

**Impact:**

- The report cannot be trusted for any overdue assessment.
- A client viewing this report will see contradictory information on the same page, eroding confidence in the system.

**Required action:** Align the Overdue Amount KPI calculation and the aging bucket logic to use the same query, date reference, and data source. Both must agree on what is overdue.

---

### Finding 6 — Period Cash and the Payment row may represent the same transaction *(Needs Clarification)*

**Location:** Period Cash KPI card and the Payment transaction row

**Observation:**

The summary cards show:
- **Period Cash: PKR 200,000** — described as cash collected during the period (typically the cash portion of a sale).
- **Period Payments: PKR 200,000** — typically a separate payment received distinct from the invoice's cash component.

The payment transaction row reads: *"Payment (cash) — Linked to #SLIP-1005"* with a value of PKR 200,000.

If Period Cash and Period Payments represent the same PKR 200,000 — i.e., the cash collected at delivery for the invoice — then the same amount is being counted in two separate KPI cards, giving a misleading impression that PKR 400,000 in total cash was received when only PKR 200,000 was.

If they are two *different* PKR 200,000 flows, then the total cash received is PKR 400,000, but the transaction table shows only one payment row, leaving the second PKR 200,000 unaccounted for in the ledger detail.

**Required action:** Clarify in the system design whether the cash portion of an invoice is recorded as a separate payment transaction or is handled inline at the invoice level. Ensure the KPI card labels accurately reflect which flows they aggregate.

## 5. Remediation Recommendations

### Priority 1 — Fix the balance formula (root cause of Findings 2 & 3)

Determine the intended ledger type. If this is an **accounts receivable** ledger (Titan sells to Al-Madina on credit):

```
Balance(t) = Balance(t–1) + Debit(Credit Sale) – Credit(Payment)
```

Where a positive balance means the customer owes Titan. Verify that the opening balance sign convention matches: if Al-Madina owes Titan, it should carry as a positive (debit) balance.

After correcting the formula, recompute the closing balance and cross-verify against all transaction rows.

### Priority 2 — Audit and complete invoice line items (Finding 1)

Run the following diagnostic against the database:

- Fetch all line items where `invoice_id = 'SLIP-1005'` and compare the count and sum against what is rendered in the ledger.
- Confirm the sum of `(cartons × price_per_carton)` for all rows equals the invoice header total of PKR 500,000.
- If additional line items exist, fix the rendering query. If no additional rows exist, the invoice total is wrong and must be corrected at source.

### Priority 3 — Fix the aging algorithm (Findings 4 & 5)

The aging query must:

1. Begin with the **opening balance as of the period start date**, not just transactions within the period.
2. Classify the opening balance against its original invoice dates to place it in the correct aging bucket (likely 90+ days for an opening balance carried from prior periods).
3. Use a **single consistent reference date** for all overdue calculations, ensuring the KPI Overdue Amount card and the aging bucket totals are computed from the same source.

Verify: `SUM(all aging buckets) = Closing Balance` after fixes are applied.

### Priority 4 — Clarify Period Cash vs. Period Payments (Finding 6)

Document in the system's data model whether:

- Cash sales are recorded as invoice entries (cash portion) only, or also as separate payment transactions.
- The Period Cash KPI card and Period Payments KPI card draw from the same or different tables.

Update the KPI card labels to be explicit (e.g., "Cash at Delivery" vs. "Post-Delivery Payments") to eliminate ambiguity for the end user.

### Priority 5 — Add a reconciliation assertion before report generation

Before the ledger PDF/view is rendered, the system should assert:

```
SUM(aging buckets) == Closing Balance        → error if false
Overdue Amount == SUM(non-current buckets)   → error if false
SUM(line item amounts) == Invoice Total      → error if false
```

Surfacing these as hard errors at generation time will prevent incorrect reports from being delivered to distributors.
